# From Evidence to Synthesis: Teaching Example

The papers, pages, and numbers in this example are fictional and must never enter a real library or final manuscript.

## Reading evidence

Paper P reports in Table 2 on PDF page 4 that, with dataset D, 100 training examples, and equal tuning budgets, A reaches 82% accuracy and B 78%. No confidence interval is reported. This supports “A reports higher accuracy in this setting”; it does not establish universal superiority or statistical significance.

Paper Q's Table 3 on PDF page 6 uses dataset E, 1,000 examples, and different tuning budgets and reports A at 84% and B at 85%. Changes in dataset and budget prevent attributing the difference from P to sample size alone.

## Cross-paper synthesis

Is A's advantage stable? The rankings differ, so the evidence does not establish general superiority. Data, sample size, or tuning may explain the difference, but neither study isolates those causes. Search next for controlled comparisons using the same data and budget across sample sizes.

## Defensible manuscript prose

A reported higher accuracy than B in the small-sample setting on D, whereas an experiment on E found the opposite ranking. Because datasets and tuning conditions differ, these results do not establish whether the advantage changes with sample size. Controlled comparisons across settings remain necessary.

## Unsupported wording and why

“A significantly outperforms B on small-sample tasks.” This broadens the scope and asserts unreported statistical significance.

“Large samples cause A to deteriorate.” This treats differences between studies as causal evidence.

“No one has studied sample-size effects.” Two sources without a comprehensive search cannot establish a field-wide gap.

## Recovery example

P and Q have been read and synthesized; the next action is to search for controlled comparisons. A successor needs the current synthesis, two notes, and research brief to continue. To write 82% or 78%, return to P's original table; this teaching example is never evidence.
